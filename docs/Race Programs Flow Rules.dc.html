Race Programs: column and page flow

The rules the layout uses now, with the producer's instructions set against each one.

How a race is broken up

The layout fills columns with blocks (atoms). An atom prints whole in one column, with the one exception set out under Race details. Each race is made of these atoms, in order:

  1. Meeting heading: venue and date (once per meeting, before its first race)
  2. Race head: grey bar, race number and name, claiming/group line, bonus and short notes, long notes. One atom, often 40–120pt deep
  3. Prizemoney: one atom, or two when a long breakdown puts "Set Weights." on its own line
  4. Fees: nomination, acceptance, declarations
  5. Race details: not one atom but a group. Each item below is its own atom, and a column can break between them:
    • Penalties: the heading and its first paragraph
    • Balloting Conditions: the heading and its first paragraph
    • Other conditions
    • Entries and Acceptances together: both headings and their lines, never split by a column break
    Any further paragraph under a heading is an atom of its own.
    Splitting inside the text. Penalties, Balloting Conditions and Other Conditions may split between lines across a column break, so long as each column carries at least three lines of that text. If either side would have fewer than three, the atom moves whole. The aim is to close white space at the foot of a column.

Rules and the producer's instructions

3. Meeting spacing. When a new meeting starts partway down a column, a 13pt gap (about one and a half lines) sits above its heading to separate it from the meeting before. A meeting at the top of a column gets no gap, so it lines up with the other columns. The gap counts towards what fits.NoneNo conflict.
4. No stranded meeting heading. A column can't end on a meeting heading; it moves to the next column.NoneNo conflict.

6. No lone carry-over. If only one atom of a race would go to the next column, the atom before it goes too, except when that atom is the only thing in the column.
Example: column 2 holds just one huge race head. The race's last atom doesn't fit and goes to column 3. The race head stays in column 2, or the column would be empty.
Don't carry one line to another column; keep it together if only one line is left or carried.Partly. The rule counts atoms, not lines, and only checks the carried side. It can move ten lines to protect one atom, and a single line can still be left at a column foot. Change: at least two lines of a race on each side of a column break.
7. Pages. Every four columns make a page. There is no difference between a column break and a new page: column 4 to the next page follows exactly the same rules as column 1 to column 2, so a race can start at the foot of column 4 and finish on the next page.A page turn follows the same rules as a column break.Complies. Confirmed after v14: a race may carry from column 4 onto the next page, with the three-line split, the two-line minimum and Entries with Acceptances all applying.

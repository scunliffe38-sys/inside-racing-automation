THE COVER FIX
-------------
A photograph proportionally deeper than an A4 page has its crop driven by the
height, so the whole image height is already on the page and there is no
vertical overflow left to slide. The horse printed where the camera put it,
head into the masthead, and no framing figure could move it.

Such a photograph now prints slightly under full size, sat on the page foot.
The subject drops clear of the masthead and the strip left at the head is
painted in a colour sampled from the picture's own top edge, so the sky reads
as continuous behind the wordmark. The width pays for it — the crop was
discarding about 13% of it anyway, which caps the reduction at that much.
Landscape covers keep the existing modest enlargement.

August's cover is unchanged: its subject sits at 48-77% of the height,
already well clear of the masthead.

THE RED ROWS
